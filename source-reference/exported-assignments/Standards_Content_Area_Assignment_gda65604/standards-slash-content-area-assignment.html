Standards to Practice: Design for Skill Transfer

Design challenge: A standard should shape what learners practice and demonstrate—not merely appear on a plan.

Part 1: Select and explain

Choose three relevant health content areas. Briefly explain why each matters for a specific group of learners.

Part 2: Connect

Select two 2024 health education standards. Explain how each develops health literacy within one of your content contexts.

Part 3: Design one sequence

For one standard/content pairing, include: learner and context, student-friendly outcome, skill cues, teacher model, guided practice, feedback method, independent application, evidence of learning, and one privacy/inclusion support.

Part 4: Defend and revise

Explain why the sequence is skills-based rather than content-only. Identify one likely implementation challenge and one revision or support that addresses it.

Success criteria

  • Standard, practice, and evidence are aligned.
  • Learners—not the technology or product—do the important cognitive work.
  • The sequence includes feedback and transfer.
  • Participation does not depend on sharing personal health information.
