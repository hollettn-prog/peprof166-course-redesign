Why Skills-Based Health Education? From Message to Design

View Sarah Benes's “Why Skills-Based Health Education?” presentation, then show how its central claim changes an instructional decision.

Respond

  1. Central claim: In two or three sentences, explain what the presentation says health education should help learners do.
  2. Evidence: Identify two ideas from the presentation that support that claim.
  3. Contrast: Describe how a content-heavy lesson and a skills-based lesson might approach the same health topic differently.
  4. Redesign: Transform one recall-focused task into a brief model → guided practice → feedback → independent application sequence.
  5. Professional shift: Name one belief or teaching habit you would keep, question, or change—and why.

Success criteria

  • Your explanation is accurate and concise.
  • The redesign gives learners meaningful practice.
  • The task does not require personal health disclosure.
  • Your reflection is connected to a specific instructional decision.
