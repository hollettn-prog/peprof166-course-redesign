What Do PE, Health, and Adapted PE Teachers Do?

Learning Materials

Meet three teaching areas

These short examples are fictional. For now, just notice what students practice and what the teacher does to help.

Physical education: learn through movement

Students practice sending a ball toward a target. The teacher demonstrates, gives them time to try, and offers one useful tip. Students try again.

Notice: The students are learning to control a movement, not simply staying active.

Health education: practice a life skill

Students practice how to respond when a friend pressures them to do something they do not want to do. The teacher models a respectful response, then students try their own wording.

Notice: The goal is to use a communication skill, not just memorize a definition.

Adapted physical education: make learning accessible

A student has trouble tracking a small ball. The teacher offers a larger ball and adjusts the distance so the student can practice sending and receiving.

Notice: The teacher changes how the student accesses practice while keeping the learning goal in view.

Try one teaching decision

A teacher says, “Everyone played a game for 15 minutes.” What would you want to know to decide whether students learned something? Think about a skill students practiced and what the teacher could observe.
